Start with the yard, not the brand

Cordless leaf blowers have reached the point where many homeowners no longer need gas for routine fall cleanup. But the biggest CFM number is not automatically the best purchase.

The right blower depends on how much ground you cover, dry versus wet leaves, typical work time, and whether you already own compatible batteries.

CFM vs MPH

CFM measures the volume of air a blower moves. MPH measures how fast the air moves. High CFM helps move broad piles; airspeed helps loosen stubborn or damp debris. Choose from airflow, airspeed, controllability, battery capacity and weight together—not one headline number.

Match the blower to your property

Small yard, patio or driveway: roughly 250–450 CFM can be adequate for dry leaves and hard surfaces. Typical suburban yard: 500–650 CFM is a strong starting point. Large yard/heavy cleanup: consider 650–800+ CFM, larger batteries or a backpack design. Wet leaves: favor stronger airflow and airspeed while accounting for shorter runtime under turbo use.

Your battery ecosystem matters

If you already own RYOBI 40V, Greenworks 60V/80V, EGO 56V, Milwaukee M18 or DeWalt batteries, staying within that platform can save meaningful money. A slightly stronger blower can be a worse overall purchase if it forces you to buy another charger and batteries.

ChoiceMeadow verdict

For most homeowners, start around the 500–650 CFM class and move upward only when property size, wet leaves or heavier debris justify it. The best value is usually powerful enough for your hardest regular job and shares batteries with tools you already own.
